## Why a call auction for one exact mint

The hypothesized job is to match contemporaneous opposite orders for the **same mint and token program** before both orders take a dealer-facing route. Whether enough such orders arrive at useful size is **U**; no buyer, seller, distributor or maker has committed (canonical E17–E18). A call auction creates a single time interval and a single, deterministic CROSS price for all matched user volume; pre-funding makes settlement obligations independent of a dealer's credit decision. It does not ensure fills, lower cost, confidentiality or a fair reference to the underlying equity. xStocks' issuer-defined multiplier and trading restrictions make the exact mint/extension-state identity economically important ([xStocks dividends and stock splits](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/dividends-and-stock-splits.md), retrieved 2026-09-28; canonical E5). Alternative issuer tokens that track the same ticker MUST NOT net or cross.

The common **one-minute** epoch is a proposal, not an optimized cadence. Shorter windows reduce waiting and cancellation exposure but thin opposite-side overlap; longer windows increase potential match volume while increasing stale-limit, issuer-event and late-order risk. A 60-second window and the order cap (provisional scale target 64, hackathon MVP ≤ 8, neither benchmarked; canonical §3.6) bound program work, not liquidity. If observed contra arrival is thin, shortening the window makes the market less useful; if users cannot tolerate the wait, lengthening it will not fix the job. Compare measured fill probability and net executable prices by time-of-day and exact mint before tuning. The external tape in canonical E13 suggests off-hours depth could be particularly weak; it is secondary dated evidence, not Kerb volume.

## Alternatives against this narrow job

| Alternative | What it does better | Why V1 does not adopt it | What would change the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous central-limit order book | Immediate executions, public queue/price discovery, finer-grained amendments | Sparse exact-mint two-sided flow can leave stale resting orders; frequent priority races and cancels incur on-chain operations. Call batching sacrifices immediacy for an opportunity to aggregate coincidence of wants. [INFERENCE] | Observed low waiting tolerance and ample standing orders; a CLOB could be more suitable. Archer's reported move from dual-flow batching to CLOB is a serious counterexample, not a differentiation claim (canonical E7; [Archer reporting](https://solanafloor.com/news/archer-exchange-launches-clob-public-beta-amidst-prop-amm-spoofing-concerns), retrieved 2026-09-28). |
| Bilateral RFQ / JupiterZ | Immediate maker response and potentially superior route when maker hedge/inventory is available; JupiterZ's documented maker orderbooks and last look are a real substitute | RFQ intermediates every user fill with a maker; it does not first net this epoch's independently funded buyer and seller. Maker optionality remains in residual, without post-close last look. This is not an RFQ-replacement guarantee. | Maker quotes routinely beat the net CROSS outcome or no simultaneous contra flow exists. [JupiterZ v2 documentation](https://jupiterz.jup.ag/docs/v2), retrieved 2026-09-28 (canonical E1). |
| AMM / concentrated liquidity | Continuous accessibility and composability; alternative routes may be much deeper | Liquidity and price are inventory curves, with LP impermanent-loss/adverse-selection exposure. Uniform CROSS is not equivalent to a swap against an LP. | Executable AMM routes net of slippage/gas/fees routinely outperform, or no counterparty arrives. |
| Oracle-referenced midpoint | Price follows off-chain/reference market, can reduce bidder/asker strategic price-setting when feed is timely | **Rejected in V1:** stale/off-hours, discrepant issuer-adjusted units or manipulable feeds can redistribute surplus between the two users while still inside limits. Using a feed to decide `p*` would add a trusted economic actor and an unverified price/market-session dependency. | An independently qualified feed with corporate-action/session/freshness/correction rules and observed user value; would be a new version, not a hidden fallback. |
| Solver-chosen price in max-volume interval | Solver can optimize another objective | Granting discretionary surplus allocation invites favored-user or side selection. On-chain recomputation of the marginal-plateau midpoint is simpler to audit. | A public, tested objective with explicit governance/revision process and convincing measured benefit. |
| Pro-rata at every level or pure time priority | Simpler allocations or faster first-arrival incentive | V1 uses price priority to reward more competitive limits, pro-rata **only at the marginal level** to share scarce fills, then a deterministic tie key for indivisible lots. | Measured manipulation from strategic splits or systematically harmful priority racing. |

An oracle-free marginal-plateau midpoint is **not** an outside-market valuation or an execution-quality certification. Users choose price bounds; the program only finds volume-maximizing integer-tick interval `[p_lo,p_hi]` and rounds its center half-even (canonical §3.3). A very wide interval reflects weak price discovery. The absence of an oracle removes one feed-specific surplus-redistribution surface but leaves user-order manipulation and stale external reference risk.

## Why uniform CROSS and discriminatory RESIDUAL

CROSS volume consists solely of users' pre-funded orders, so one uniform price prevents different fill prices among users who trade in the same primary batch, conditional on their limits and discrete allocation. For an excess side, a best price limit receives priority, then the marginal level is pro-rated; it is **not** a fully incentive-compatible mechanism. Any strategically set bound may change both eligibility and the marginal-plateau midpoint. Mandatory publication of order details/on-chain state means no claim of hidden flow or confidentiality.

The funded maker phase begins only **after** primary allocations are fixed and stored, is optional per user order, executes at each maker's level price, and cannot change CROSS volume or `p*`. Different residual users may pay/receive different maker prices: it would be misleading to call the combined result a uniform-price auction. The user opts in at placement to residual, and the receipt exposes `CROSS` versus `RESIDUAL`, maker price, lots, fee and remaining refund. Makers escrow every level and cannot decline a favorable post-close fill; they bear inventory/hedging risk. A maker may decide not to quote the next epoch. This is not a maker commitment or a cost guarantee.

**Why not uniform residual at the primary clearing price `p*`?** An imbalance has no opposing user at that size, so forcing an external market maker to quote at `p*` could eliminate residual supply; allowing the maker to declare a funded ladder makes its inventory price explicit. **Why not let makers participate in `V*`?** That would disguise dealer-facing volume as peer coincidence and could let a maker move the primary price. See the ordered matching rules and illustrative numerical examples in [`01-MECHANISM-SPEC.md`](01-MECHANISM-SPEC.md).

## Manipulation and accepted risks

| Vector | Mechanism / exposure | V1 response and remaining risk |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap spoofing or cancellation before close | Large stated limits can widen or move the maximum-volume interval; cancellations may create false apparent depth before close. | Full prefunding costs capital and cancellation requires `Open`; final order set, not off-chain indications, sets outcome. This does **not** prevent same-epoch transient display spoofing or cancel-and-repost racing; distributor must label pre-close book *indicative*. [INFERENCE] |
| Self-crossing | One controller pre-funds both sides to influence `V*`/`p*`, generate prints, or acquire a share of prorated fills. | Two-sided fees and escrow make it costly, not impossible, especially across wallets. No verified beneficial-owner identity, no invented wash-trade prevention. Compliance analysis and surveillance need independent treatment ([`../legal/09-REGULATORY-AND-LEGAL.md`](../legal/09-REGULATORY-AND-LEGAL.md)). |
| Epoch-end sniping / validator ordering | A participant observes final visible limits and inserts an order just before close, or targets the tie-break using transaction inclusion order. | Explicit `close_ts`, `placed_slot`, nonce and owner determine deterministic allocation once the final set is fixed. Public pre-funded orders, slot ordering and 60-second epochs do **not** prevent time-bandit ordering or cross-slot sniping; compare routes and study penalties, commit-reveal only as a later design. |
| Marginal-order splitting | Dividing an order into several owners/nonces can gain more leftovers under per-order one-lot tie distribution. | Funding/fees impose some cost; still accepted as a V1 fairness risk. A distinct-owner limit would not establish economic independence. Quantify via adversarial tests and reconsider allocation if material. [INFERENCE] |
| Maker ladder gaming | Maker posts both sides before close, withdraws while `Open`, or shapes the visible residual menu. | Maker levels fully pre-funded, cannot cancel or last-look after close; residual explicitly separate, and user may opt out. Active pre-close ladder is still cancelable, so display is indicative. |
| Issuer action / display multiplier | Issuer updates multiplier, pauses/freezes accounts or rotates authorities; raw lots retain bytes but can change economic display or transferability. | V1 **refuses any mint with a set permanent delegate or a `TransferHook` extension on either leg** (hook program set or unset). [INFERENCE] A mint without `TransferHook` cannot gain it later under Token-2022 initialization rules; re-verify against the exact token-program version. Hook support requires a future reviewed, audited amendment. Fingerprint rechecks at `open_epoch`, `close` and `clear`, plus multiplier, pause and vault-solvency guards, still matter. Cleared nominal entitlements never reverse, but an issuer-caused shortfall marks the affected leg `Impaired` and stops payouts; frozen vault can make even refund impossible. No automatic recovery or pro-rata. See [`../architecture/05-TOKEN-2022-ISSUER-INTEGRATION.md`](../architecture/05-TOKEN-2022-ISSUER-INTEGRATION.md). |
| Data/route cherry-picking | A gateway compares Kerb result with stale or unavailable external route and presents hypothetical saving as executed saving. | Label quotes executable/observed versus unavailable, timestamp and amount; no best-execution claim. See [`../architecture/06-OFFCHAIN-SERVICES.md`](../architecture/06-OFFCHAIN-SERVICES.md). |

The design is a testable market hypothesis. A no-cross epoch is not a failed safety property; it is evidence against the economic use case if repeated. Evaluate this mechanism against actual executable alternatives with the same mint, size, route eligibility, time and fees, not against an invented universal spread. The evidence register and forbidden claims remain binding ([`../00-CANONICAL.md`](../00-CANONICAL.md) §§4–5).
