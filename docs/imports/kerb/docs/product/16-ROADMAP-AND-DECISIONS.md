## ADR log

The following records contain **Context → Decision/option → Consequences → Revisit trigger**. ADR-001–008, ADR-010 and ADR-011 describe proposed design choices requiring owner review, not acceptance, audit, launch authorization or deployed software; ADR-009 is explicitly **Proposed/Open**. The proposed canonical contract wins on conflicts as an editorial consistency reference, not an approval.

### ADR-001 — Name the mechanism Kerb

- **Context:** The source idea is row #5 “Dark Pool (spot tokenized stocks)” ([origin](../sources/notion/idea-bank-row-5.md)); that title suggests a privacy property this V1 does not have. A short product name must not smuggle in a confidentiality claim.
- **Decision:** Use **Kerb** and the one-liner “Cross tokenized-stock orders directly.” The historical kerb-trading reference explains the name (canonical §1). Identify exact mints in technical material, not tickers.
- **Consequences:** Positioning centers user-to-user crossing before dealer residual. Trademark, domain, handle and Colosseum project-name availability remain **U / not checked**; legal clearance and naming checks are distinct.
- **Revisit trigger:** A conflicting mark, unavailable contest name or misleading market connotation; rename only with synchronized citations, code and pitch, not a parallel alias.

### ADR-002 — No privacy, BAM or TEE in V1

- **Context:** Public Solana placement exposes order intent; the source row offered RFQ, TEE, rollup or BAM interpretations. BAM maker priority sequences enrolled maker updates; it is not a confidentiality layer (canonical E6).
- **Decision:** Plain on-chain pre-funded orders and deterministic auction. No privacy, anonymity, dark-pool positioning, TEE, BAM, MagicBlock or Arcium integration in V1. A later commit–reveal option needs measured leakage and its own threat model.
- **Consequences:** The order set may leak before close; toxic-flow/manipulation risks remain. Separate issuer-aware execution economics, not hidden order flow, is the hypothesis. The [pitch](../pitch/13-PITCH-NARRATIVE.md) must never make forbidden claims.
- **Revisit trigger:** Direct evidence that public intent leakage harms eligible users enough to outweigh reveal latency, nonreveal and implementation risk; approval to revise canonical V1 semantics explicitly.

### ADR-003 — Oracle-free program-computed marginal-plateau midpoint clearing

- **Context:** A solver-selected or external reference price can reallocate trade surplus even if volume is maximum; oracle freshness is another trust boundary. Compute targets are **provisional and unbenchmarked**: hackathon MVP ≤8 user orders/epoch, 1 maker, 1 level/side; scale ≤64 orders and 8 levels/side × 4 makers only pending LiteSVM/SBF CU and transaction-size results. Each `settle_order`/`settle_maker` call after `Cleared`, or `refund` call in `RefundOnly`, handles at most one recipient leg; `refund` is never callable after `Cleared`.
- **Decision:** On integer ticks `p ∈ [L_min,L_max]`, maximize `min(D(p),S(p))`. With `V*>0`, the maximizing set is a contiguous interval `[p_lo,p_hi]`: `p_lo` is a submitted ask limit, `p_hi` a submitted bid limit. Set `p* = round_half_even((p_lo+p_hi)/2)`, so `V(p*)=V*`; no cross and endpoints/price absent if either side empty or `V*=0`. Fill exactly `V*` at that uniform price; eligible bids have limit ≥`p*` and asks ≤`p*`; price priority and marginal pro-rata with `(placed_slot, nonce, owner)` leftover-lot ordering. Program sorts and sweeps distinct submitted limits in O(n log n), or O(n) after verifying a crank sorting hint; it never scans every tick or trusts a crank's price. See [mechanism](../mechanism/01-MECHANISM-SPEC.md).
- **Consequences:** No external price feed sets primary price, but the price can differ from any external executable quote; limit orders and order-submission games still matter. Marginal-plateau midpoint tie rounding must be property-tested; CU and transaction-size bounds remain unproved until benchmarked. An oracle-free clearing rule is **not** a claim of best execution or measured savings.
- **Revisit trigger:** Production evidence of economically harmful manipulability or unsustainable compute that cannot be mitigated without changing price semantics, followed by explicit versioned review. Solvers cannot change the proposed V1 result.

### ADR-004 — Residual maker is separate and discriminatory

- **Context:** Unbalanced users need a possible second liquidity source, but allowing maker levels into primary clearing would distort `V*`/`p*` and obscure which price the user received. JupiterZ already provides maker RFQ (E1); this phase is a different fallback design, not a claimed superior quote.
- **Decision:** After primary fills are fixed, only opted-in unfilled user remainder can trade against signed, epoch-specific, fully funded maker levels posted while Open. User limit must be met; maker level price applies and may differ across fills. Priority: user best limit then `(placed_slot, nonce, owner)`; maker best price, and across same-price makers the marginal-lot rule of canonical §3.3 step 6 applies (proportional to **remaining level quantity**, floored, leftover lots by `(posted_slot, maker)`). Label each receipt `CROSS` or `RESIDUAL`.
- **Consequences:** No last look after close; maker inventory/risk must be escrowed. Residual cannot move primary price or fill. Disclose distinct price, wait and fee effects; residual makers have no committed participation (E18 U).
- **Revisit trigger:** Net comparisons show the optional phase harms opted-in users, makers refuse pre-funding, or separate quote economics do not justify maintenance. Removing it would require an explicit canonical cutover, not silently altering V1.

### ADR-005 — Full pre-funding and no minimum fill

- **Context:** Unfunded orders, last look and minimum-fill constraints complicate atomicity, liveness and allocation in short epochs. Token-2022 raw balances, quote token-program variation and issuer powers make settlement and recovery central risks. A permanent delegate can remove vault assets without changing the mint extension hash; V1 must not assume it can pay claims after a shortfall.
- **Decision:** Buyer locks `qty_lots · limit_ticks · tick_quote + max_fee` quote atoms, with `max_fee = ceil(5 · qty_lots · limit_ticks · tick_quote / 10_000)`; seller locks `qty_lots · base_lot_raw` raw atoms. No min-fill, hidden quantity, amendments or post-close cancellation. Market binds `base_token_program` and `quote_token_program` independently, refuses **any mint with a permanent delegate**, and uses isolated per-epoch PDA-owned base and quote vaults. On `clear`, fix nominal entitlements; *pre-clear* deadline/ordinary guard failure → RefundOnly with withdrawal of original deposit only if issuer and token program permit it. *Post-clear* `settle_*` deterministic preflight checks entitlement-fixed vault, mint and canonical recipient account: frozen vault/recipient, paused mint or missing/closed canonical account → successful `SettlementBlocked(reason, leg, slot)` with no transfer, unchanged Unpaid entitlement and advisory, non-sticky marker. Wrong caller-supplied destination, mint or program hard-reverts, never blocks. A later call always rechecks; the first passing preflight plus successful transfer settles the leg, and a missing canonical account can be recreated permissionlessly. Unexpected CPI failure after preflight reverts and is surfaced as an **off-chain incident**, with entitlement intact. There is **no `mark_settlement_blocked` instruction**. An issuer-caused vault shortfall on **either** side of clear marks the affected leg `Impaired`: emit `VaultShortfall`, stop its payouts, preserve nominal claims (**original deposits pre-clear**), allow other-leg settlement. Kerb offers no redistribution, automatic recovery or restitution; impaired claims may never be recoverable. Each user order's aggregate CROSS + RESIDUAL notional `N` incurs **one** proposed `ceil(5N/10_000)` fee; buyer pays `N + fee`, seller receives `N − fee`. Split 6/3/1 with each share floored and remainder dust in fee vault. See [canonical §3](../00-CANONICAL.md) and [security](../security/07-THREAT-MODEL.md).
- **Consequences:** Capital stays tied up while orders wait; partial fills are possible. An issuer freeze or pause may indefinitely prevent payout; a shortfall creates insolvency for one isolated epoch leg without altering nominal holder claims, while unaffected leg may settle. **Rejected alternative: pro-rata distribution of remaining funds.** It changes promised entitlements and can depend on claim order; neither loss socialization nor a recovery waterfall is specified in V1. Any permanent-delegate mint is excluded, a material possible constraint on available xStock mints (**U** until exact mints are checked). Receipt/UI must show impairment and frozen-vault risk plainly. An issuer-caused shortfall before clear is **not** ordinary RefundOnly and a cleared result is never reverted.
- **Revisit trigger:** Measured capital inefficiency materially prevents two-sided flow, or inability to prove per-epoch vault accounting, no-socialization shortfall and conditional unaffected-leg settlement. Any delegate exception or new credit/recovery model requires an explicit canonical revision, independent audit and counsel review, not an incremental switch.

### ADR-006 — Solana first

- **Context:** The source thesis targets on-chain xStocks/Token-2022 issuer controls; Solana is the authentic initial market and World's Fair track (E15). Cross-chain scope would obscure the crucial same-mint flow test.
- **Decision:** Build the Solana localnet/devnet SYNTHETIC proof first and seek separately qualified Solana launch conditions only after evidence and counsel. Robinhood Chain/Arbitrum hackathon track eligibility and one-submission/multi-track rule are separate and E16 is U.
- **Consequences:** One runtime, one unit model and one issuer profile per qualified market; no claim of currently supported xStock mints or instant multi-chain distribution. Robinhood Chain port is a contingent later product, not V1 scope.
- **Revisit trigger:** No eligible/lawful Solana asset or distributor path, or evidence that a different chain solves the same real job; record revised thesis and rebuild rather than cosmetically adding a second chain.

### ADR-007 — No token or points

- **Context:** Artificial incentives could produce orders without a repeated execution job; token economics distract from net user value and add security/legal scope. Canonical's **proposal to test** charges five bps per filled user side with 6/3/1 between protocol/distributor/crank; makers pay zero. Thus a CROSS lot produces approximately 10 bps gross on unique cross notional and a RESIDUAL lot approximately 5 bps gross on unique residual notional, never 10 bps on residual.
- **Decision:** No governance token, points, liquidity mining or incentive-led traction in proposed V1. Model fees separately by CROSS and RESIDUAL; all projected unit economics are [INFERENCE] hypothetical sensitivities until real throughput is observed. See [business model](../business/11-BUSINESS-MODEL.md) and open [ADR-009](#adr-009--residual-fill-fee-and-rebate).
- **Consequences:** Cold start must be solved by genuine eligible contra-flow and optional pre-funded maker interest, not subsidized vanity volume. Fee may prove too high and is explicitly unvalidated.
- **Revisit trigger:** Measured behavior and counsel-supported incentive analysis show a legitimate need; do not retrofit points to hide failed demand gates.

### ADR-008 — No cross-chain matching

- **Context:** Similar tickers can represent different issuer obligations, token programs, legal rights and jurisdictions; Robinhood Stock Tokens are EVM instruments, not fungible Solana xStocks (E12). Bridging adds custody, finality and eligibility risk.
- **Decision:** Each auction's bid and ask refer to the **identical base mint address** and qualified quote mint on one chain, with the base/quote token programs recorded independently. No cross-issuer, cross-mint or cross-chain order book or synthetic equivalence. A future Robinhood Chain port sources separate buyers, sellers and maker inventory.
- **Consequences:** Fragmented liquidity remains; throughput must be earned per mint/chain. The port cannot borrow Solana demand proof without revalidation, and no cross-chain settlement is represented in the [submission](15-HACKATHON-PLAN.md).
- **Revisit trigger:** Only a new product thesis with legal, issuer, bridge security and settlement-finality proofs; explicit canonical revision required. Matching by ticker remains prohibited.

### ADR-009 — Residual fill fee and rebate

- **Status:** **Proposed / Open — undecided.** Canonical §3.5 retains a proposed 5 bps on each filled user side, including RESIDUAL, a zero-fee maker and the 6/3/1 split until this ADR is decided and the proposed contract is explicitly revised. CROSS has two charged user sides (≈10 bps of CROSS notional); RESIDUAL has one (≈5 bps of RESIDUAL notional), never two.
- **Context:** A residual user trades at a funded maker's level price, not primary `p*`. The 5-bps fee plus a distributor rebate can make routing via Kerb worse than dealing with that maker directly. Maker pre-funding and no last look are costs too; the business case must not count residual volume as if both counterparties paid.
- **Options, not decisions:** Keep the proposed 5-bps residual user fee and 3/10 distributor share; charge a **lower residual user fee** while retaining a split to be specified; retain the user fee but pay **no distributor rebate on residual** (treatment of that share must be explicitly specified); charge **zero residual fee** (and fund settlement cranks some other way or explicitly accept no fee); charge a **maker fee** instead of or alongside the residual user fee (requires maker appetite, funding/accounting and fresh legal review). None is implemented or approved. The cross fee need not change with a residual-only decision.
- **Decision criteria and evidence:** Compare actual same-mint, size- and eligibility-matched **executable direct quotes from the same maker** with residual user net at the maker level price, fee, placement/settlement network cost, latency and partial-fill risk; inspect losing as well as winning windows. Obtain written, nonbinding distributor trial economics and maker terms under each proposed fee, including funding/no-last-look acceptance. Use [business/12's preregistered bilateral net gate](../business/12-GTM-AND-VALIDATION.md#preregistered-gates-and-stop-rules-for-the-2026-10-12-decision), record referral/no-referral mix and separately model protocol, distributor and crank contribution and rounding by fill class ([business/11](../business/11-BUSINESS-MODEL.md)). Qualified counsel and security/accounting review must resolve whether a variant is permissible and fundable. No such evidence is reported; do not infer a choice from synthetic fills.
- **Consequence / revisit trigger:** Residual routing must not be advertised as improving net outcomes absent comparisons. Founder/product/economics owner records a selected variant only after evidence and owner review; update canonical §3.5, receipt/UI, escrow and all economics together. Until then no alternative silently changes the proposed schedule.

### ADR-010 — Refuse transfer-hook mints in V1

- **Status:** **Proposed — owner review required.**
- **Context:** A `TransferHook` authority can repoint the hook program, and a pinned hook program can be upgraded; reviewed code could therefore be replaced before a payout CPI. A reverting CPI is not a safety boundary against a hook that succeeds. [INFERENCE] Token-2022 extensions are fixed at mint initialization, so a mint without `TransferHook` cannot gain it later; re-verify against the exact token-program version at qualification.
- **Decision:** Reject market creation if either base or quote mint carries `TransferHook`, whether its hook program is set or unset. No hook extra accounts or hook CPI path qualify in V1. Re-check both mint extension/authority fingerprints at `open_epoch`, `close` and `clear`; an opening mismatch blocks new epochs pending requalification, a mismatch before clearing an existing epoch sends it to RefundOnly, while post-clear mismatch preserves fixed results and `settle_*` subject to freeze/pause/recipient preflight but blocks another opening until requalification. Hook support is a future reviewed amendment, blocked until audited.
- **Consequences:** The rule may exclude candidate mints (**U** until exact base and quote mints are checked). Production eligibility remains blocked; a successful synthetic profile is not a named xStock qualification. Refusal removes hook-program upgrade/repointing from V1 payout CPIs, not issuer freeze/pause or program-upgrade risks.
- **Alternatives considered:** Pinning and reviewing an exact hook program/extra-account list is insufficient if authority rotation or program upgrade replaces reviewed code. An on-chain hook-fingerprint block per leg with exit on restoration or requalification was rejected for V1: requalification needs a governance path and adds a blocked-claim state. Neither an expected CPI revert nor an off-chain incident can make a successful unreviewed hook safe.
- **Revisit trigger:** A specifically identified hook-bearing candidate mint and reviewed governance/requalification and claims-exit design, followed by independent hook-path audit, token-program-version verification and explicit canonical amendment; no implicit exception.

### ADR-011 — Reference trading-UX app with off-chain routing layer

- **Status:** **Proposed — owner review required.** Scope: hackathon reference integration and later product option; no code exists.
- **Context:** Kerb makes users wait up to one epoch (~60 s) with funds locked, and a lone order may not cross. A bare Kerb order form is therefore a poor trading experience next to immediate routes (JupiterZ, AMMs; E1). The V1 SDK only builds owner-signed instructions ([off-chain services](../architecture/06-OFFCHAIN-SERVICES.md)); it holds no funds and signs nothing. The goal of this ADR is **user and trading UX**, not distribution: an app built by the team starts with zero flow and cannot create same-mint contra-flow (E17 U).
- **Decision:** Build one reference app on the Kerb SDK with an **off-chain routing layer**: (1) order form showing exact mint address, side, size, limit, residual opt-in, maximum lock and maximum fee, plus issuer controls; (2) at placement, fetch an immediate quote from an existing route where available and *suggest* a limit no worse than that quote at that moment; (3) epoch countdown, cancel while `Open`, and a receipt separating CROSS and RESIDUAL; (4) after `settle_*` returns the unfilled remainder, offer the user a **separate, second wallet-signed** trade on an existing route; (5) route comparisons labelled only `OBSERVED_EXECUTABLE`, `OBSERVED_INDICATIVE` or `UNAVAILABLE`. Run on **SYNTHETIC** test mints until a mint qualifies. Call it a reference app, not an aggregator with flow.
- **Consequences:** No atomic fallback in V1: the remainder faces price movement during the wait, needs a second signature, and cannot be rerouted at all while an issuer freeze, pause or `Impaired` leg holds it. The suggested limit bounds the Kerb fill against a quote that may have expired by fallback time; it is not a best-price or savings claim. If the team's app names itself as distributor, it collects the 3/10 rebate on top of the protocol 6/10; this must be disclosed to users. Fallback trades on external routes earn Kerb nothing; any router referral or platform-fee program is **U** until each router's terms are read. Success metrics: CROSS fill rate over app orders, bilateral net outcome of CROSS versus same-time external quotes, and cancel/abandon rate during the wait. A near-zero CROSS rate is evidence against E17, not an app defect.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) **Maker bridge**, a bot posting residual ladders and hedging on external routes: rejected for V1; pre-funding with no last look carries inventory risk, and a team-run bot is proprietary trading with conflicts needing counsel ([09](../legal/09-REGULATORY-AND-LEGAL.md) Q22). (b) **Program CPI into a router during `clear`**: rejected; it breaks the rule that residual never alters the stored primary result (ADR-004), adds CU and external-code risk. (c) **Listing Kerb as a venue inside an existing router**: not pursued; routers integrate immediate quotes, Kerb is a 60 s call auction, and router venue requirements are **U**.
- **Revisit trigger:** Business/12 gates show repeated same-mint flow and a distributor asks for integration; or measured wait/abandon data shows a different UX is needed. An atomic fallback or maker bridge requires a new ADR, canonical amendment, security review and counsel input.
