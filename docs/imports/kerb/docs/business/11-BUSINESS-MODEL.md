## Who pays, who routes, who takes risk

Kerb is a proposed execution venue, not an issuer or inventory principal. The **customer/channel is the distributor**—a wallet, broker or app with legally eligible, existing tokenized-stock order flow. Its user is the buyer or seller; it must show them execution choices and bear its own integration/support/eligibility responsibilities. The **proposed, unvalidated economic payer is each filled user order**, at 5 bps on the order's aggregate `CROSS + RESIDUAL` filled notional. A CROSS lot has two charged user sides (approximately 10 bps of unique cross notional); a RESIDUAL lot has one charged user side (approximately 5 bps of unique residual notional), because the fully funded maker pays zero. A named distributor receives 30% of the fee collected on its referred order before rounding; permissionless settlement cranks receive 10% under a fee-attribution policy still open in canonical §3.9. This schedule and rebate, especially on residual, must be tested against direct access to the same maker, not assumed viable.

There are **no committed counterparties or distribution agreements (U, E18)**. The channel would need both sides in the **same mint, chain, qualified extension state and epoch**. **Production eligibility remains BLOCKED:** no exact mainnet xStock mint has yet been checked against the canonical allowlist, and no named issuer token is represented as Kerb-supported. Any demonstration uses **SYNTHETIC** test mints. A marketplace effect for one Solana mint does not carry to another issuer, another mint with the same ticker, HyperCore's wrapped representation or Robinhood Chain. A distributor pair means two independent order sources willing to examine the same mint/window, not a claimed integration ([canonical production guard](../00-CANONICAL.md#37-issuer-aware-guards-the-wedge), [market analysis](10-MARKET-AND-COMPETITION.md), [GTM](12-GTM-AND-VALIDATION.md)). Off-hours demand and improvement are **U, E17**.

## Fee arithmetic: no doubled notional

Let `N` be one **matched** buyer/seller `CROSS` notional measured once in USDC value: not `2N` because two users participated. Ignoring atomic rounding for this basis-point illustration, each user pays `N × 5/10,000`; the pair pays `N × 10/10,000 = 10 bps × N` gross. When both have a distributor, protocol receives `N × 6 bps`, their referrers jointly `N × 3 bps`, and settlement cranks `N × 1 bp`. These splits total **10 bps**, not a 10 bps protocol take. If neither side has a named distributor, the foregone 3 bps goes to protocol; its share becomes **9 bps**, not 6 bps. If just one has a distributor, protocol receives **7.5 bps**, the one distributor **1.5 bps**, and cranks **1 bp**.

These are continuous-notional approximations. **At the atom level** the fee is charged **once per user order** on aggregate filled quote-atom notional `N_order = N_cross + N_residual`: `fee_order = ceil(5 × N_order / 10,000)`. A bid pre-funds `qty × limit × tick_quote + ceil(5 × qty × limit × tick_quote / 10,000)`; after allocation a buyer pays `N_order + fee_order` and a seller receives **exactly** `N_order − fee_order`. Protocol/distributor/crank shares are each floored separately and the remainder remains emitted dust in the fee vault. Do **not** round cross and residual independently for a partly crossed order, count dust as operating revenue or assume percentage exactness at small sizes ([canonical fees](../00-CANONICAL.md#35-fees-proposal-to-test-not-market-validated)).

A `RESIDUAL` fill of user notional `R` has **one charged user**, the maker pays 0. If that user order is entirely residual, gross is approximately `R × 5 bps`; if referred, protocol `R × 3 bps`, distributor `R × 1.5 bps`, crank `R × 0.5 bps`, and without referrer protocol `R × 4.5 bps`, crank `R × 0.5 bps`. If the *same order* receives both cross and residual, compute its **one** aggregate fee first, then attribute revenue across fill types by a clearly disclosed convention; no additive rounded fill-level fees. Residual executes at the maker's level price, not primary `p*`; **5 bps plus a distributor rebate on residual can make Kerb worse for a user than trading that maker directly**. Test direct-maker quotes after fees and latency before routing. A cross/residual blend cannot be assigned a flat 10 bps gross on unique aggregate notional. Report gross, protocol take, rebates and crank payouts with actual per-order fee ledgers ([canonical residual/fees](../00-CANONICAL.md#34-residual-phase-separate-cannot-change-primary-outcome)). ADR-009 in [the roadmap](../product/16-ROADMAP-AND-DECISIONS.md#adr-009--residual-fill-fee-and-rebate) leaves this policy open: lower residual fee, no distributor rebate on residual, zero residual fee and a maker fee are options, not current fees. Canonical retains 5 bps per user side and zero maker fee pending a decision.

**Two independent break-evens must hold:**

1. **User route:** for side `s`, a simplified saving in bps at comparable prices is `alternative all-in cost bps_s − 5 bps − Kerb incremental network fee bps_s`. More generally `saving_s = (alternative effective buy cost − Kerb effective buy cost)` for buyers and `saving_s = (Kerb effective sell proceeds − alternative effective sell proceeds)` for sellers, divided by each side's appropriate notional. This includes actual price differences between the alternative and program-computed `p*`, alternative venue/network fees, Kerb placement/settlement/refund costs, rounding, and size/partial fill. In particular, no oracle participates in Kerb's clearing price; the arithmetic fee saving alone cannot establish price improvement. A filled `RESIDUAL` compares its maker price plus 5 bps with the user's executable alternative. A no-cross, expired or blocked order has zero realized execution saving, still costs time/network fees, and may tie up escrow. Neither users nor distributors should be routed solely for a fee rebate if user net is worse.
2. **Company:** with annual referred `CROSS` notional `N_c`, referred `RESIDUAL` notional `N_r`, annual fixed costs `F` and variable cost `v_c, v_r` in bps per notional (excluding already-split crank/rebates), contribution `= N_c × (6 − v_c)/10,000 + N_r × (3 − v_r)/10,000 − F`. Break-even requires this to be non-negative. For a cross-only model with variable cost `v` and `F > 0`, required annual `N_c ≥ F × 10,000/(6 − v)` if `v < 6`; at or above 6 bps variable cost, more matched volume does not solve the margin. Contractually funded audit/legal and customer-support costs are not free because settlement is on-chain. Distributor acquisition and compliance may not scale linearly with notional. Discounts or new revenue streams are **not** in the V1 model.

**[INFERENCE] Hypothetical customer comparison:** Suppose each side's executable route imposes a 12 bps all-in cost relative to the *same comparable benchmark*, Kerb's realized cross price introduces zero benchmark disadvantage, and Kerb's incremental network cost is 2 bps per side at that order size. Then hypothetical saving is `12 − 5 − 2 = 5 bps/side`. All figures and zero price-disadvantage assumption are **hypothetical, not observed**. If the alternative is 5 bps and Kerb network 2 bps, the same arithmetic is `5 − 5 − 2 = −2 bps/side`: do not route. If Kerb's price is even 6 bps worse for the buyer than the comparison benchmark, the first buyer's 5 bps arithmetic advantage becomes a 1 bp loss. The seller's benefit must be tested independently. Quote staleness, auction wait and inability to fill are extra decision costs not condensed into a fabricated bps number.

Revenue requires *fills*, not potential volume. If matched-pair rate is `m` of eligible routed user-side notional and fill mix remains cross-only, then unique executed cross notional is roughly `m × routed user-side notional / 2` **only when both sides' submitted sizes balance and the same dollars are counted on both sides**. Residual notional instead counts one filled user side against a maker, not half that amount. Measure the actual ledger instead; cancellations, price incompatibility, pro-rata partials, refunds, disabled mints and paused sessions defeat a simple funnel. `RefundOnly` is **pre-clear only**; withdrawal of the original deposit is possible only where the token program and issuer permit transfer. After clearing, fixed entitlements do not revert: deterministic preflight may record `SettlementBlocked` without transfer and leave permissionless retry possible, whereas an unexpected CPI error rolls back and is surfaced off-chain. Any vault shortfall instead marks the affected leg `Impaired`, stops all its payouts, leaves nominal entitlements unchanged and emits `VaultShortfall`; the unaffected leg keeps settling. A pre-clear impaired escrow owner retains a nominal claim to the original deposit, which may never be recoverable. There is **no automatic pro-rata or restitution** and no V1 recovery path. V1 refuses a permanent-delegate mint at market creation; a future reviewed exception requires audit and counsel before release. A fee-bearing match is not necessarily paid out: report accrued fees, paid entitlements, blocked legs and impairment distinctly. These are issuer/legal/operational contingent liabilities, not recovered/refunded orders ([canonical lifecycle and shortfall](../00-CANONICAL.md#37-issuer-aware-guards-the-wedge)).

## The venture-scale test, without a TAM slide

Volarević's [$100M revenue / $1B valuation lens](../sources/x/status-2038643299221729462.md#4-colosseums-investment-thesis) is founder commentary, **not Colosseum's official threshold or a valuation model**. **[INFERENCE] Cross-only hypothetical:** at **10 bps gross on matched CROSS notional**, `$100 million ÷ 0.001 = $100 billion` annual CROSS notional, or about `$8.33 billion/month`. Only **6 bps protocol revenue** is retained if both sides are referred, so `$100 million ÷ 0.0006 = $166.67 billion` annual CROSS notional, about `$13.89 billion/month`. **[INFERENCE] Residual-only hypothetical:** at **5 bps gross** on RESIDUAL notional, the gross hurdle is `$100 million ÷ 0.0005 = $200 billion/year`; at **3 bps retained protocol** with a referred user side, the protocol hurdle is `$100 million ÷ 0.0003 = $333.33 billion/year` (about `$27.78 billion/month`). For any mixture, retained revenue is `(6 × annual CROSS + 3 × annual RESIDUAL) / 10,000` with both classes referred; there is no 10-bps residual multiplier. All hurdles exclude variable costs, small-order rounding and concessions; a higher residual share or lower residual fee worsens the retained-revenue hurdle under this proposal. A fully un-referred theoretical cross take of 9 bps needs `$111.11 billion/year` to reach $100 million, but a scale story premised on distributor flow cannot rely on having no distributors. A $1B valuation at $100M revenue is a **10× revenue multiple assumption**, not a promised exit.

For scale context only, secondary research on **all** sampled Solana xStock trades over 2026-06-29→07-28 reports about **$342.5 million in thirty-day traded notional**, concentrated in a few tickers and large wallets ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-tokenized-stocks-xstocks), retrieved 2026-09-28; E13). The **[INFERENCE] hypothetical cross-only** $13.89B monthly *Kerb retained-revenue hurdle* is ~**40.5×** that observed market-wide monthly figure; a referred residual-only hurdle is larger, not another 10-bps scenario. Periods, venues, definitions and possible wash/market-maker turnover are not fully comparable, and this is **not** a forecast. It does mean the current observed Solana xStock market cannot support this VC outcome at the proposed fee even if Kerb captured every trade as an eligible matched cross; the sector would have to expand substantially and Kerb would have to capture a meaningful share of *genuine opposing flow*. A venture case needs a defensible, evidence-based expansion path (more eligible exact mints and distributors, perhaps separate future chains after qualification), not an assumption that one mint proves $100M revenue. Porting to another chain splits rather than pools matching liquidity.

## Cost of obtaining a dollar of protocol take

The following costs exist even before scale and are **not measured or budgeted commitments**: senior on-chain/security engineering, custody and issuer-extension review, external audit, legal opinions/licensing and jurisdiction-specific distribution controls, integration engineering and support for each distributor, RPC/indexing/log retention, monitoring and incident response, multisig governance, referral accounting and reconciliation, maker onboarding, and payment of network/priority fees where the business sponsors them. An always-on quoting service carries off-hours coverage risk; a pre-funded maker carries inventory/funding opportunity cost and will demand sufficient expected residual flow. xChange's issuer maker guide documents restricted onboarding, 24/5 operation and weekend unavailability ([xChange maker guide](https://docs.xstocks.fi/developers/xchange-solana-market-maker-guide.md), retrieved 2026-09-28; E3); a maker's rational outside option may be superior. Cranks earn a percentage of *collected* fees, so a zero-fill epoch offers no fee bounty; liveness and RPC sponsorship must be modeled rather than assumed.

**[INFERENCE] Hypothetical fixed-cost stress test, not actual team or spend:** three full-time people at $150,000 annual loaded cost each ($450,000), external legal/compliance $200,000, audits/security review $150,000, infrastructure/operations/support $200,000 → **$1 million/year** hypothetical fixed cost. If additionally the protocol funds a net **1 bp** of variable routing/operations per executed `CROSS` dollar (hypothetical cross-only case) and retains 6 bps before that spend, contribution is 5 bps; hypothetical break-even is `$1,000,000 / 0.0005 = $2 billion annual CROSS notional` (about $166.67M/month). Residual is **excluded** from this calculation and would contribute only `(3 − v_r)` bps per referred residual notional, not the cross rate. If actual costs differ, substitute verified costs; there is no actual hiring plan or paid revenue to insert here. Higher legal/security spend, limited permitted jurisdictions, fee pressure or support per failed epoch can move the bar sharply.

**Moat test:** As exact-mint liquidity concentrates, an additional distributor can improve counterparty probability for *that* mint/window and attract more flow—conditional network effects, not a global ticker/chain moat. A larger router can internalize orders or add a crossing phase, established venues can price below 5 bps, and issuer changes can force a market halt. The only plausible defensibility is repeated qualified distribution relationships, reliable execution receipts and measured net advantage in a constrained asset set, all **U**. Regulatory classification and permission to operate a securities trading venue could be decisive ([legal analysis](../legal/09-REGULATORY-AND-LEGAL.md)). Kill the business thesis if repeat same-mint opposing flow is absent, alternative net outcomes dominate after fees, distributors cannot legally route, makers will not pre-fund the residual, or realistic volume cannot support compliance/security costs. The [preregistered tests](12-GTM-AND-VALIDATION.md) decide whether even a narrow experiment earns further investment; no traction, revenue or full-time team is claimed.
