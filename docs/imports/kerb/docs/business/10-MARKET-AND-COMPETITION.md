## Market boundary and investment question

Kerb is a proposed Solana call auction for **one exact base mint and one USDC quote mint** at a time; each leg records its own token-program id. The base xStock is expected to be Token-2022, but the qualified USDC quote mint is not assumed to be Token-2022. Its primary phase crosses pre-funded user bids and asks at the program-computed uniform price; an optional, independently pre-funded maker fills eligible remainders **after** the cross at maker prices. It is not a private venue and does not match stock tickers, wrapped representations, or different issuers' claims interchangeably. The apparent opportunity is avoiding an intermediary's spread when opposite natural orders already exist in the same base mint and minute, not manufacturing liquidity. That flow is **U (E17)**. No users, makers or distributors have committed **(E18)**. The cost hypothesis is not yet measured; see [the validation protocol](12-GTM-AND-VALIDATION.md) and [mechanism rationale](../mechanism/02-MARKET-DESIGN-RATIONALE.md).

The investor question is whether a routable, repeatable *distribution channel* can deliver enough opposing same-mint flow, with a measured two-sided net improvement after fees, to support a material business. An isolated auction demo does not answer it. Josip Volarević's [Colosseum playbook](../sources/x/status-2038643299221729462.md) urges a startup rather than hackathon lens, direct customer conversations, a clear team, competition and a plausible category winner; these are an author's heuristics, **not official scoring rules**. Colosseum's [own guide](https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/) (retrieved 2026-09-28) favors founders building potentially viable businesses, while its [World's Fair rules](https://colosseum.com/legal/Crypto%20World's%20Fair%20Hackathon%20Rules.pdf) (retrieved 2026-09-28) actually specify functionality, impact, novelty, UX, open source and business plan. The $100M revenue/$1B valuation frame is a venture hurdle, **not a forecast**; [unit economics](11-BUSINESS-MODEL.md) makes it explicit.

## The accessible landscape as of 2026-09-28

- **Solana:** xStocks are Token-2022 tracker certificates with Scaled UI Amount; raw token atoms and displayed exposure diverge as the issuer's multiplier changes. They do not confer shareholder voting rights and are not offered to US persons ([issuer legal overview](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/product-legal-overview), [corporate actions](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/dividends-and-stock-splits.md); both retrieved 2026-09-28; E4–E5). **Kerb production eligibility is BLOCKED:** no exact mainnet xStock mint has passed the canonical extension/authority allowlist, so the presence of Solana xStocks does not mean any is supported; demonstrations must use **SYNTHETIC** test mints ([canonical guard](../00-CANONICAL.md#37-issuer-aware-guards-the-wedge)). Technical transferability is not permission to operate a venue. Solana order-flow substitutes include RFQ and AMM/prop-AMM routes. xChange is the issuer's issuance/redemption RFQ for **onboarded clients**, not a holder-to-holder matching book; its issuer-facing quote window is about 60 seconds and spreads vary by session ([xChange](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/issuance-and-redemption/atomic-rfq-xchange.md), [maker guide](https://docs.xstocks.fi/developers/xchange-solana-market-maker-guide.md); retrieved 2026-09-28; E2–E3). It is unavailable on weekends per the maker guide, while AMMs may remain routable if the specific pool and transfer rules permit.
- **HyperCore:** xStocks announced native USDC spot books for NVDAx, SPYx, QQQx, SKHYx and MUx on 2026-08-10; these named assets are **competitor examples, not Kerb-qualified Solana mints**. This is an existing order-book alternative for an eligible trader willing and able to hold/bridge the relevant representation; the issuer itself describes wrapping and bridging between Solana, HyperEVM and HyperCore ([xStocks announcement](https://xstocks.fi/news/xstocks-goes-live-on-hyperliquid-bringing-the-markets-deepest-tokenized-equities-framework-to-traders), retrieved 2026-09-28; E11). HyperCore depth, fees, eligibility and bridge-inclusive execution at any given size/window have **not** been independently measured here. A HyperCore asset is not the same Solana mint and cannot enter a Kerb Solana auction without a separate asset conversion. Kerb cannot position itself as the missing tokenized-stock order book on Hyperliquid.
- **Robinhood Chain:** Robinhood describes a permissionless EVM-compatible L2, native Stock Tokens and a mainnet RPC. Its support page says these tokens are Jersey-issued debt securities without underlying shareholder rights and states US-person restrictions ([Robinhood support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-chain-mainnet/), retrieved 2026-09-28; E12). Holder eligibility, actual transfer constraints and executable secondary books are **U**. A future port is a new chain/issuer/token standard with separate legal, product and liquidity validation, not pooled Solana flow ([roadmap](../product/16-ROADMAP-AND-DECISIONS.md)).

**Counterevidence to the attractive off-hours story:** Bitquery's secondary analysis of Solana xStock trading from 2026-06-29 through 2026-07-28 reports weekend volume around 16% of weekday volume, about 61% of weekday volume during NYSE hours and 96.3% concentrated in ten tickers. It also finds heavy large-wallet turnover, which is not evidence of consumer opposing orders ([Bitquery investigation](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-tokenized-stocks-xstocks), retrieved 2026-09-28; E13). One period and its methodology do not establish Kerb's addressable matched flow. Flow Traders describes fragmented maker pre-funding, off-hours risk and the inability of makers to create end-user demand; this is an operator's perspective, not an independent demand survey ([Flow Traders, 2025-07-03](https://flowtraders.substack.com/p/tokenization-in-capital-markets-a), retrieved 2026-09-28; E14). **[INFERENCE]** Focus one high-volume mint and one observed overlap window first; do not assume weekend demand just because transfer is possible.

## The actual substitutes, by decision-maker

- **Buyer/seller already holding the exact mint:** Immediate JupiterZ RFQ, a routed Raydium/Orca pool, a prop-AMM quote, or, on another chain after conversion, a HyperCore book. JupiterZ v2 streams maker orderbooks and grants a maker last look before settlement ([JupiterZ v2](https://jupiterz.jup.ag/docs/v2), retrieved 2026-09-28; E1). Raydium documents mint-specific pool discovery and routing, and Orca documents swap routing through pools and third-party aggregators ([Raydium integration](https://docs.raydium.io/integration-guides/aggregator), [Orca swap](https://docs.orca.so/trade/how-to-swap); retrieved 2026-09-28). Those routes may win on immediacy, certainty, aggregation and depth. Kerb could win only for an eligible, filled user-user CROSS whose realized net proceeds/cost improve **on both sides** after the proposed 5 bps per charged user side, transaction costs, delay and partial-fill risk. A RESIDUAL fill charges only its one user side 5 bps; the maker pays zero, and a direct route to the same maker could outperform Kerb after that fee/rebate. If there is no opposite flow, an instant quote is the superior product for an impatient trader.
- **Wallet/app/broker distributor:** An existing router integration may be cheaper operationally than adding a 60-second, pre-funded escrow and settlement lifecycle **[INFERENCE]**; costs have not been measured. A distributor would consider Kerb only if repeat user outcomes exceed integration, support and compliance burden; a 30% share of collected fees to named referrers is an *unvalidated proposal*, not evidence of an agreement. V1 refuses any mint with a permanent delegate; a future reviewed exception needs audit and counsel. If an issuer-caused vault shortfall occurs despite guards, the affected epoch leg becomes `Impaired`: payouts stop, nominal claims remain, and pre-clear claims equal original deposits; there is no V1 recovery path and claims may never be recoverable. A frozen vault or paused mint can also keep transfers blocked indefinitely, despite permissionless calls ([canonical issuer guard](../00-CANONICAL.md#37-issuer-aware-guards-the-wedge)). Brokers may need licenses or issuer permissions that an API does not cure ([legal analysis](../legal/09-REGULATORY-AND-LEGAL.md)). Route quality must be benchmarked against the alternative actually available to *that* user/mint/jurisdiction, not a generic displayed midpoint.
- **Maker/desk:** xChange can source/redeem inventory for onboarded makers during its hours, often bundled with a user fill; JupiterZ offers high-frequency streamed quoting with last look ([maker guide](https://docs.xstocks.fi/developers/xchange-solana-market-maker-guide.md), [JupiterZ v2](https://jupiterz.jup.ag/docs/v2); retrieved 2026-09-28). Kerb requires signed, fully escrowed quotes before close, pays makers no taker fee, and denies last look. This may be attractive for measured residual flow but ties up capital and exposes the maker to stale off-hours quotes. No maker has indicated willingness **(E18)**. The crank is an open settlement caller, not the liquidity supplier.
- **Issuer/onboarded institutional client:** xChange's atomic issuance/redemption and known issuer quote have a different economic job from secondary holder matching; xChange may beat Kerb on firm execution, inventory replenishment and regular-hours depth. Kerb neither controls issuer action nor replaces corporate-action, halt or eligibility restrictions. The [issuer integration plan](../architecture/05-TOKEN-2022-ISSUER-INTEGRATION.md) is a prerequisite, not an advantage already delivered.

## Prior art: what each does better, and the conditional Kerb wedge

This is not a feature-score matrix. Mechanisms differ by assets, users, chain and latency; a rational router can select an incumbent or no trade.

- **Jump DFBA; Archer C4:** Jump's 2025-08-21 dual-flow batch auction proposal separates maker/taker flow into two fast, independent uniform-price auctions; maker orders do not cross other makers. Archer's Cypherpunk Sep 2025 Colosseum submission adopted dual-flow batching, won 4th DeFi and joined C4; secondary reporting later describes Archer's CLOB public beta ([Jump paper](https://jumpcrypto.com/resources/dual-flow-batch-auction), [Archer project](https://colosseum.com/arena/projects/archer-exchange), [SolanaFloor report](https://solanafloor.com/news/archer-exchange-launches-clob-public-beta-amidst-prop-amm-spoofing-concerns); retrieved 2026-09-28; E7). **They win** on explored maker/taker microstructure and, for a CLOB, continuous discovery. **Kerb could win** by making user/user same-mint cross the *first* phase and only exposing unmatched opt-in flow to makers, with issuer-aware safeguards. **Prove** that this actually obtains contra-flow without losing customers to faster books.
- **Blackpool/Darklake C2; Urani:** Radar Sep 2024 Blackpool/Darklake won 2nd DeFi, joined C2 and pursued ZK-protected trading; Colosseum now describes Darklake as acquired in 2026 ([Colosseum portfolio](https://colosseum.com/companies/darklake-1), retrieved 2026-09-28; E8). Renaissance Mar 2024 Urani won 1st DeFi & Payments with intent aggregation and MEV protection ([Colosseum project](https://colosseum.com/arena/projects/urani), retrieved 2026-09-28; E9). **They win** for protected intent, aggregation or confidentiality where their implementations and asset support apply. **Kerb could win** at a much narrower issuer-specific contra-flow job without claiming protection or confidentiality. **Prove** economically relevant exact-mint overlap; the award history alone does not demonstrate a gap.
- **CoW; Renegade:** CoW explicitly batches coincidence-of-wants and can source residual liquidity; it wins at established EVM matching/solver reach ([CoW documentation](https://docs.cow.fi/cow-protocol/concepts/how-it-works/coincidence-of-wants), retrieved 2026-09-28; E10). Renegade describes private, midpoint-pegged crosses and lists Arbitrum/Base venue endpoints ([Renegade](https://renegade.fi/), retrieved 2026-09-28; E10). Renegade wins on a confidentiality requirement that Kerb does **not** address. **Kerb could win** for one eligible Solana Token-2022 issuer mint if its raw-unit, multiplier and pause handling plus measured route outcomes matter. **Prove** issuer compatibility and cost after delays; crossing itself is established prior art.
- **Kinetic SUPA:** Its documentation describes slot-level uniform-price AMM batching and a pool-reserve-based clearing price; it lists first SUPA pools and limit-order support as planned, not verified production for any xStock ([Kinetic documentation](https://docs.kinetic.xyz/liquidity/supa-amm), retrieved 2026-09-28). **It wins** on AMM depth and faster batches *if* pools and routing are available. **Kerb could win** when directly overlapping limit orders clear without LP inventory in its one mint. **Prove** usable depth and realized all-in price against any live SUPA route; neither the existence of documentation nor a planned pool is evidence of a live xStock market.
- **Jump BisonFi; Raydium/Orca:** Jump describes BisonFi as a rapidly repriced prop-AMM with off-chain prices and on-chain books, distinct from its DFBA *proposal* ([Jump, 2026-04-15](https://jumpcrypto.com/resources/propamms-and-the-next-chapter-of-permissionless-market-structure), retrieved 2026-09-28). Raydium/Orca provide continuous AMM liquidity and direct/routed swaps ([Raydium](https://docs.raydium.io/integration-guides/aggregator), [Orca](https://docs.orca.so/trade/how-to-swap); retrieved 2026-09-28). **They win** on immediate execution and amortized distribution/market-maker infrastructure. **Kerb could win** only when a filled opposite pair beats their executable quote *net*, not by citing SOL/USDC prop-AMM statistics for xStocks. **Prove** same-mint venue coverage, fees and matched-size comparison.
- **JupiterZ; xChange; HyperCore spot:** JupiterZ wins on router distribution and streamed firm-seeking RFQ, though maker last look exists; xChange wins on eligible issuer issuance/redemption and inventory sourcing; HyperCore wins for traders already on its live tokenized-stock CLOB ([JupiterZ](https://jupiterz.jup.ag/docs/v2), [xChange](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/issuance-and-redemption/atomic-rfq-xchange.md), [HyperCore announcement](https://xstocks.fi/news/xstocks-goes-live-on-hyperliquid-bringing-the-markets-deepest-tokenized-equities-framework-to-traders); retrieved 2026-09-28; E1–E3, E11). **Kerb could win** on same-mint Solana secondary buyer/seller crosses *before* a maker spread is paid. **Prove** that distributor flow routes in, fills within the window and improves both counterparties' outcomes. An issuer RFQ, Solana secondary swap and HyperCore wrapped-book trade are not identical eligibility or settlement alternatives.

## Why examine this now; what would falsify the investment thesis

**Verified:** Solana xStocks exist with a corporate-action multiplier; on-chain RFQ/AMM alternatives exist; the issuer has documented a maker sourcing flow; HyperCore lists native spot xStock books; Robinhood documents an EVM Stock Token chain; antecedent batch and private crossing structures already exist (sources above, retrieved 2026-09-28). **[INFERENCE]** Those developments make a narrowly qualified Solana secondary-matching experiment timely, not unique. **Unverified:** repeat opposite end-user flow, available eligible routes per jurisdiction, economically significant spread avoidance and any distributor contract. The Bitquery concentration/off-hours evidence pushes the prior *against* a large standalone weekend venue.

A category business would require a durable way to aggregate order flow **within each chain and exact mint**, better outcomes than smart routing, repeat distributor adoption, defensible issuer-asset handling and a viable licensing/operating posture. The same ticker on different issuers/chains adds no automatic matching density. If conversations produce only hypothetical interest, quote windows rarely improve both sides, a maker refuses pre-funding, or regulatory requirements preclude operation, the case fails; do not rebrand this as a generic tokenized-stock exchange.
