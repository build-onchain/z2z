## 2. Candidate uniform-price call auction [I; D0 reference, not selected production rule]

**Question tested:** Can a bounded set of independently accepted buys and sells produce a deterministic, limit-respecting maximum-lot *peer cross* without exposing each order to an individual matcher? The *integer objective below is a candidate*; neither owner choice nor MPC provider constitutes adoption. Compute solely on the complete accepted set of one `(pair_id, epoch_id, rules_hash)`. A non-executable D0 simulator may use synthetic orders and fee-free integer math; it proves arithmetic behavior only, **not** private execution or funded settlement.

For each integer tick `p` in the interval from the smallest to largest submitted live limit:

```text
D(p) = sum(q_i for accepted BuySPLwithZEC orders with limit_i >= p)
S(p) = sum(q_i for accepted SellSPLforZEC orders with limit_i <= p)
V(p) = min(D(p), S(p));     V* = max_p V(p)
```

If either side is empty or `V*=0`, return **no cross, no clearing price and zero fills**, not an inherited maker quote. Otherwise let `p_lo` and `p_hi` be smallest/largest maximizing ticks; `p* = round_half_even((p_lo+p_hi)/2)`. Let `s=p_lo+p_hi`, `k=floor(s/2)`: even s→k; odd s→k if even, else k+1. This lies in `[p_lo,p_hi]` and trades V*. No oracle establishes fair value; strategic limits can move interval. Historical D0 may show p* **to researchers only**; the later candidate seals participant own fill/price and coordinator allocation with **no public exact clearing price default**. Public auxiliary/output inference is still a hard gate, not solved by sealing.

Every accepted buy with limit `≥p*` and sell with limit `≤p*` is eligible. Allocate **exactly** `V*` lots on *each* side. For this candidate, process buys by descending limit and sells by ascending limit; fill full price levels in that order until the final oversubscribed marginal level. At that level with quantities `q_i`, total `Q`, and `R < Q` lots still to fill, assign `floor(R × q_i / Q)` per order, then award the remaining whole lots one at a time in ascending deterministic tie-key order. A possible private tie key is `H("Kerb/D0/tie", pair_id, epoch_id, rules_hash, canonical order commitment)` computed within an independently controlled matching computation; how commitments are formed and whether their keys can be ground, biased, selectively omitted or safely shown is [U]. **It is not a fairness proof or approved production priority**. A fallback to `(placed_slot, owner)` would leak identity/timing and is **not** permitted. The two sides' allocations are paired for individual settlement evidence without changing their allocated quantities, price or filled-set root. Sealed fill records would bind the exact accepted root, rule hash, order commitment, filled lots, price, notional, per-leg destination commitments and unique trade/settlement IDs; defining independently verifiable output proofs and who learns pairing/identity remains [U]. Cluster signatures can authenticate a candidate output but cannot alone prove complete admission, rule execution, funding or Ironwood payment.

**Why a bounded sweep suffices [I, mathematical claim to test]:** `D` is non-increasing and `S` non-decreasing as `p` rises. If two ticks maximize positive `V`, every tick between them also has `D≥V*` and `S≥V*`, so maximizing ticks form one interval. Its lower endpoint occurs at an ask limit and upper endpoint at a bid limit; otherwise moving one tick outward would keep both qualifying quantities at least `V*`, contradicting the endpoint. Evaluating `V` at **sorted distinct submitted limits** is therefore enough to recover both endpoints; midpoint need not itself be a submitted tick. A reference implementation may sort `O(n log n)` and sweep `O(n)`; it need not loop across a large numerical tick gap. A future privacy-preserving circuit might use a different fixed-size algorithm/capacity; these computational claims are not benchmarks or Solana compute-unit estimates.

**Concrete D0 vector (synthetic, zero fees, `lot_raw=100`, `tick_zat=1`):** buy `B1: 4 lots @ 6`, `B2: 2 @ 5`; sell `A1: 3 @ 4`, `A2: 2 @ 5`. At `p=4`, `V=3`; at `p=5`, `V=5`; at `p=6`, `V=4`, so `p_lo=p_hi=p*=5`. Buys hypothetically receive `B1=4`, `B2=1` lots; sells hypothetically deliver `A1=3`, `A2=2` lots. Five lots × 100 atoms = **500 SPL atoms** of matched obligations; 5 lots × 5 zatoshis = **25 zatoshis** of hypothetical matched ZEC obligations, **not actual transfers**. `B1` is allocated 20 and `B2` 5 zatoshis against hypothetical 24/10-zatoshi buy maxima; **unused hypothetical ZEC custody credits of 4/5 would not be self-refundable**. `B2` has one unfilled lot, but its future actual credits and refund state would remain ledger liabilities, not freely spendable change. A seller's unfilled locked SPL could be refundable only in an allowed escrow state and if token transfers work. This vector is arithmetic, **not** a claim of a working deposit/credit or atomic transfer. Every future fee schedule, partial-fill settlement and per-order netting policy would require corresponding new vectors and proof.

## 3. Invariants and proof obligations before any funded market [I/U]

| Obligation | Checkable requirement and failure behavior |
|---|---|
| **Accepted-set integrity and privacy** | Every executed fill references one nonduplicated receipted ciphertext in the agreed root; no omitted/disputed input, invalid signature, double-spent credential or unlinked funds. Show an adversarial trace giving a matcher all accessible logs/metadata and prove/test it recovers **neither plaintext nor participant identity**. If input missing or gate fails: no funded fill; abort/reconcile. |
| **Pair and amount binding** | Same cluster/mint/program, network/pool, receiver, epoch/rules/order/hold generation through admission/fill/release/refund. Each entitlement **unit/portion** at most one reservation; multiple disjoint portions from one credit may back separate obligations, never same units reused across orders/pairs. Buyer FULL max debit and seller atoms held from PREPARE through canonical root resolution despite no Fill records; only finalized allocation or irreversible fenced canonical ABORT/exclusion releases. Reject overflow/unit mismatch/overfill/duplicate authorization before either leg. |
| **Auction feasibility** | For all `i`, `0 ≤ fill_i ≤ qty_i`; `Σ filled buys = Σ filled sells = V*`; buys have `limit_i≥p*`, sells `limit_i≤p*`; every filled lot uses the same candidate `p*`. When `V*=0`, no cross or price is fabricated. Reordering an identical accepted set leaves reference output unchanged; adversarial order-commitment grinding/tie-key bias must be measured. |
| **Per-pair conservation** | Before/during/after matching, buyer `C = available + admission_prepared + epoch_encumbered + seller_payout_outstanding + buyer_unused_outstanding + seller_payout_final + buyer_refund_final`; seller `locked total = available/locked + admission_prepared + epoch_encumbered + filled_outstanding + final_unfilled_returnable + released + returned`. All disjoint; no max-debit release during delayed matching. Final M,N splits N filled and M−N unused, not new credit. Pending payout/refund stays outstanding until actual final effect; original inputs and change are separate inventory counted once, never duplicate credit. Candidate protocol fee zero, sponsor fee capital separate; future fees need separately revised invariant. Reorg/impairment/unsolicited assets require pause/reconcile, not phantom availability. |
| **Cross-chain non-atomicity** | Authorization must bind pair, trade, previous/next state, recipient/amount, expiry and one-time nonce. `Authorized` is not `Paid`; track both chains' sent/final/reorg/disputed states and prohibit a refund/release race with an already-sent other leg. SPL-first exposes the seller to missing ZEC; ZEC-first exposes the buyer to missing SPL. Quorum collusion/offline/freeze can strand funds; stop/dispute rather than claim automatic rollback. |

## 4. Separation from alternatives and from legacy mechanism

A maker-visible **bilateral RFQ** is an execution comparator, not equivalent private batch matching: a maker reads a request and may decline/reprice subject to the RFQ's own commitments. [JupiterZ's documented RFQ](https://jupiterz.jup.ag/docs/v2) [P for vendor documentation, accessed 2026-09-29] and [NEAR's chain support](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/chain-support) [P for vendor claim, accessed 2026-09-29; Zcash transparent-only in the source-spec review] do not prove direct shielded Ironwood↔SPL atomicity. A switch to maker-visible RFQ would change the owner's no-single-matcher design and needs a new decision. A **continuous order book** trades immediacy for visible sequence/priority pressure and independently unresolved admission/settlement. A **transparent Zcash HTLC** [ZIP 300](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0300) [P, accessed 2026-09-29] changes the chosen shielded custody product. None is a silent failure fallback.

**Not inherited:** old Solana-only same-mint `CROSS` / prefunded residual maker ladder, `(placed_slot, nonce, owner)` priority, 5-bps-per-side protocol/distributor/crank splits, one-minute epoch, maker last-look assumptions, unconditional refund branch or `Cleared` state machine. This draft specifies **only** a candidate peer-to-peer batch cross; `V*=0` produces no fill. Adding an inventory-backed residual route would need separate maker quote/escrow/credit rules across *both* chains, ordering and failure policy, a privacy test for maker-visible flow, fee math and fresh approval; it cannot be smuggled into `V*` or the example. Whether a call auction beats RFQ or no new venue is an empirical question, not decided by the algorithm. See [market rationale](02-MARKET-DESIGN-RATIONALE.md) and the preserved [Idea Bank row #5](../../docs/sources/notion/idea-bank-row-5.md) [P for author hypothesis, captured 2026-09-28; not user-demand evidence].
